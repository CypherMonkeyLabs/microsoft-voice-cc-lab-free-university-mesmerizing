Glossary

Core terms from this campus. Each in-app entry also has a config note and a fault. Accessed 14 October 2026.

  • Microsoft 365 tenant — The boundary that holds Entra ID, licenses, Teams policies, and verified domains for one organization.
  • Verified domain — A domain added and proven in the tenant. Direct Routing SBC FQDNs must live on one of these domains.
  • onmicrosoft.com domain — The default tenant domain. It is not a supported domain for a Direct Routing SBC FQDN.
  • Homed online — Direct Routing users must be homed in Microsoft 365, not left on a Skype for Business on-premises registrar.
  • Islands mode — A coexistence mode. Direct Routing is not supported for users in Islands mode.
  • Entra ID — The identity directory for the tenant. Teams users, resource accounts, and admin roles are Entra objects.
  • Resource account — A disabled-for-sign-in Entra account that represents an auto attendant or a call queue. It is not a human login.
  • Cloud Voicemail — The Teams voicemail service. It deposits messages in the user’s Exchange mailbox.
  • Teams Phone — The license capability that lets a user use Teams as a phone system. It is not, by itself, a PSTN carrier.
  • Calling Plan — A Microsoft-operated PSTN option. Domestic, international, and pay-as-you-go are the usual flavors. It is an extra license on top of Teams Phone.
  • Audio Conferencing — The license that provides dial-in numbers for meetings. It is not Teams Phone.
  • Teams Premium — An add-on for advanced meetings and related features. It does not replace Teams Phone or a Calling Plan.
  • Teams Phone Resource Account license — The license each auto attendant or call queue resource account needs, even when the account has no phone number. Subscriptions with Teams Phone include a zero-cost allotment you still have to acquire.
  • Pay-as-you-go Calling Plan — A Calling Plan billed on use. Also the funding pattern, with communications credits, for some voice-app outbound calls.
  • Communications credits — A balance used when a call is not covered by a Calling Plan minute. Voice apps use it for some outbound cases.
  • Shared Calling — A pattern where users place outbound PSTN calls through a resource account’s number instead of each having a number.
  • Common area phone — A shared handset. On Direct Routing it does not need a Calling Plan license.
  • Teams Rooms license — Rooms use their own SKU family, not a borrowed user Calling Plan. Names such as Basic and Pro change. Check the current name.
  • Conditional Access — Entra policy that can block a phone or a room that is not in the trusted device set.
  • Shortest media path — Microsoft’s network guidance prefers a short path to the Microsoft network, without a proxy in the media path.
  • VPN and real-time media — Microsoft recommends that you avoid sending real-time Teams media through a VPN. QoS on the VPN does not fix the extra delay.
  • ExpressRoute is optional — Teams does not require ExpressRoute. Local internet egress is the usual design. If you do use ExpressRoute, follow the current network article rather than assuming media is happier there.
  • QoS markers enabled — The Teams admin center setting that allows clients to insert DSCP. Without it, a correct ACL still sees unmarked client traffic from managed endpoints that honor the policy.
  • Three to five hops — Microsoft’s planning note: keep the client-to-network-edge path short, on the order of three to five hops, and the ISP-to-Microsoft edge short as well.
  • Layer-3 G.711 — G.711 at 20 ms is 160 bytes of payload plus 40 bytes of IP, UDP, and RTP. That is 80 kbit/s per direction.
  • Ethernet G.711 — Add an 18-byte Ethernet header to the G.711 20 ms packet and the figure is 87.2 kbit/s per direction.
  • Operator Connect — A certified carrier manages PSTN for you inside the tenant. You need Teams Phone. You do not need a Microsoft Calling Plan or your own SBC.
  • Operator Connect for India — The India-specific operator model. It exists because India PSTN rules are not the same as a generic Operator Connect deployment.
  • Teams Phone Mobile — The user’s mobile SIM number is also their Teams number, through a certified mobile operator. Teams Phone is required.
  • Direct Routing — You connect a certified SBC to Teams and bring your own carrier. Users need Teams and Teams Phone. They must be homed online. Islands mode is not supported.
  • Mixing PSTN options — One tenant may use Calling Plans, Operator Connect, Teams Phone Mobile, and Direct Routing at the same time for different users.
  • SIP proxies — For commercial Microsoft 365, signal to sip.pstnhub.microsoft.com, then sip2, then sip3, on TCP 5061. GCC High and DoD use different names.
  • Media ports for Direct Routing — UDP 3478–3481 and 49152–53247 between the SBC and the media processors, unless media bypass keeps the media off that path.
  • Media re-targeting — Microsoft does not support changing the media target in the middle of an active Direct Routing call.
  • Two media ports per call — Microsoft’s planning tip: allocate at least two media ports on the SBC for each concurrent call.
  • Normalization — The dial plan rewrites what a person dialed into a number the routes can match, usually E.164. The first matching rule wins.
  • Tenant dial plan — A set of normalization rules you assign to users. A user can have a more specific plan than the tenant default.
  • PSTN usage — A name that links a voice routing policy to one or more voice routes. The policy’s ordered list of usages is the permission to place those calls.
  • Voice route — A number pattern plus an ordered list of SBC gateways. It does nothing until a PSTN usage on the user’s policy points at it.
  • Voice routing policy — The per-user list of PSTN usages, in order. Teams walks that order when it looks for a route.
  • Gateway priority — Each voice route lists SBCs with a priority. Lower priority number is tried first in this campus’s teaching model. Confirm the current article before you depend on a tie.
  • Emergency short code — 999, 112, and 911 must be normalized on purpose. A general rule that runs first can swallow them.
  • Auto attendant — A voice app that answers, plays a greeting, and routes by schedule, menu, directory, or operator.
  • Call queue — A voice app that holds callers and offers them to agents by attendant, serial, round robin, or longest idle routing.
  • Conference mode — A queue behavior where the caller stays in the conference while agents are tried, so the transfer is faster and the greeting is not replayed the same way.
  • Overflow — Where the call goes when the queue is full or the timeout expires. It can be a person, another voice app, voicemail, or an external number.
  • Directory search — An attendant feature that lets a caller reach a person by name or extension.
  • Holiday schedule — A calendar the attendant uses to play the closed greeting. It is not the same object as business hours.
  • Dynamic emergency calling — Teams picks a civic address from the network location it can see: trusted IP, site, and subnet in the usual design.
  • Civic address — The street address you validate for emergency calling. A dispatchable location is specific enough for responders.
  • Trusted IP — An address that means ‘this client is inside our network.’ It is how Teams decides the client is on a site you mapped.
  • Emergency calling policy — Controls notification and how the client looks up location. It is not the PSTN route.
  • Direct Routing emergency — You, the SBC, and the carrier deliver the emergency call. Microsoft does not become the emergency carrier because Teams is the client.
  • Calling Plan emergency — With Microsoft Calling Plans in supported countries, Microsoft’s emergency calling service is part of the plan. You still configure locations.
  • Location-based routing — Stops a user in a regulated site from placing a PSTN call through a gateway in another site. It is a toll-bypass control.
  • Survivable Branch Appliance — A Direct Routing appliance that can keep local PSTN calling when the branch cannot reach Microsoft 365.
  • Media bypass — Media can flow between the Teams client and the SBC, skipping Microsoft’s media processors. Signaling still goes through Teams.
  • Local media optimization — A Direct Routing pattern with internal and external SBC interfaces so a branch user stays on the local media path.
  • SIP Gateway — Microsoft’s front end for certain compatible analog and SIP devices that are not Teams phones.
  • Teams-certified phone — A handset in Microsoft’s certified device program, signing in as a Teams user.
  • SRTP — The media encryption Direct Routing uses. Signaling is TLS. A successful INVITE with plaintext RTP is not the design.
  • TLS 1.2 — Direct Routing signaling expects modern TLS. TLS 1.0 is not a compatible offer.
  • Certificate SAN — The name on the SBC certificate must match the FQDN you paired. Wildcards are allowed when they follow RFC 2818.
  • Microsoft trusted root — The SBC certificate must come from a CA in Microsoft’s trusted root program. A private lab CA is not enough for production pairing.
  • Server Authentication EKU — The certificate should include the server authentication extended key usage.
  • Call Analytics — The per-user, per-call quality view in the Teams admin center. Use it when one person had one bad call.
  • Call Quality Dashboard — The aggregate quality report. Use it for subnets, buildings, and trends.
  • Poor stream clues — Teach yourself to look at jitter, packet loss, and round-trip time separately. A mark of DSCP 0 plus high jitter is a different fault from 12% loss with EF still set.
  • SIP ladder — A time-ordered picture of requests and responses between client, proxy, SBC, and carrier, joined by Call-ID.
  • One hypothesis — Change one thing, then collect the same evidence again. A reboot plus a dial-plan edit plus a firewall change teaches nothing.
  • Dynamics 365 Contact Center — Microsoft’s contact-center product. It can stand alone or sit with Customer Service. Voice uses Azure Communication Services under the covers.
  • Customer Service workspace — The representative experience in Dynamics. Contact Center can embed there or run as its own product surface.
  • Workstream — The intake pipe in unified routing. It classifies work and sends it to a queue.
  • Work classification — Rules that set skills, intent, or priority on a work item before assignment.
  • Capacity profile — How many items of each type a representative can hold. A voice call usually consumes more than a chat message.
  • Representative — The human on the contact-center side. AB-250 calls the console the representative experience.
  • Copilot in the contact center — Assistive summaries, suggested replies, and knowledge inside the representative experience. It is not the PSTN route.
  • AB-250 channels weight — The published AB-250 study guide weights channel implementation at about 30–35 percent, the largest slice.
  • Connect model — A certified contact center attaches to Teams through certified SBCs and Direct Routing.
  • Extend model — The contact center uses the Teams client, Graph, and Cloud Communications APIs. Teams Phone is the calling system and the agent works in Teams.
  • Unify model — The model Microsoft Learn names, as of 14 October 2026, for a native experience. Partner material describes it as ACS-based applications on Teams calling infrastructure. Older decks say Power. Ask which current model a vendor holds.
  • Teams Phone extensibility — A way for a contact center, including Dynamics 365 Contact Center, to use a customer’s existing Teams Phone for the agent’s telephony.
  • CUBE as a bridge — Cisco Unified Border Element can be the certified SBC between Teams and CUCM or a carrier. The IOS-XE release you run has its own guide.
  • One normalization owner — During coexistence, either Teams or CUCM should transform numbers, not both.
  • Tromboning — A call from a user who stayed on CUCM to a user who moved to Teams hairpins through the SBC. It is normal during dual-running and expensive as a steady state.
  • Webex calling bridge — A SIP trunk through a local gateway or CUBE can carry calls between Webex Calling users and Teams users during a split estate.
  • UCCE coexistence — Contact-center agents can stay on CVP and Finesse while knowledge workers move to Teams. The SBC sends contact-center numbers one way and user numbers another.
  • Genesys and Teams — Genesys sells contact-center integrations with Teams. The certification model, Connect, Extend, or Unify, decides the media path and the licenses.
  • Amazon Connect and Teams — A customer pattern uses an SBC between Teams Direct Routing and Amazon Connect or a SIP voice connector. It is a bridge you design, not a Microsoft Calling Plan feature.
  • Call Automation — The ACS server-side calling API. Your app answers, plays, recognizes DTMF or speech, and transfers. You receive events. You do not drive a desktop click.
  • Calling SDK — The ACS client SDK for a human in a browser or a mobile app. Different from Call Automation, which is the server.
  • Recognize step — The Call Automation action that collects DTMF or speech. It needs a timeout and a path for ‘none of the above.’
  • Azure AI Speech — Microsoft’s speech-to-text and text-to-speech service. Copilot Studio and custom IVRs can use it. It is not emergency calling.
  • Copilot Studio voice agent — A bot built in Copilot Studio that can front a voice channel, including Dynamics contact-center voice, using topics or generative orchestration.
  • Azure AI Foundry — The Azure portal family for models, agents, and evaluations. The earlier name was Azure AI Studio. It does not route PSTN.
  • Teams interop with ACS — ACS can be set up to call Teams users and to join Teams meetings when the interop prerequisites are met.
  • MS-700 — MS-700 is still live, with a skills update on Oct 27 2026; it is NOT retired and has no replacement. It is the exam for Microsoft 365 Certified: Teams Administrator Associate.
  • MB-280 retired — MB-280 retired Jul 31 2026, with AB-210 as its successor; AB-210 is in beta. It covered Sales and Customer Insights, not the contact-center product.
  • AB-210 — Dynamics 365 Sales AI Consultant Associate. AB-210 is the successor to MB-280 and is in beta. It is not a replacement for MS-700.
  • AB-250 — Transforming Contact Center Experiences with AI in Dynamics 365. It earns Dynamics 365 Contact Center AI Engineer Associate. Pass mark is 700. The public page allows 120 minutes.
  • AZ-900 — Azure Fundamentals. It was not on the retirement list reviewed on 14 October 2026.
  • SC-900 — Security, Compliance, and Identity Fundamentals. The English certification is scheduled to update on 21 October 2026.
  • MS-900 retired — Microsoft 365 Fundamentals retired on 31 March 2026.
  • AI-900 retired — Azure AI Fundamentals retired on 30 June 2026.
  • Passing score 700 — Microsoft role-based exams, including MS-700 and AB-250, require 700 out of 1000.
  • Convenience recording — A user starts a recording of a call, meeting, or event. Policy can allow it. The user can stop it. Storage follows meeting rules.
  • Compliance recording — A third-party recorder is invited by policy into new calls, meetings, and events. The user does not start it and participant consent is not the control. Notification is enforced.
  • Compliance recording application — The bot instance of a certified recorder. The policy references it. Without it, granting the policy does not record.
  • Existing calls are unaffected — When you grant a compliance recording policy, calls and meetings already up are not swept in. Test with a new call.
  • Quality management — Listening to and scoring recorded interactions. Often the same vendor as compliance recording, sometimes a separate one.
  • Workforce management — Scheduling and adherence for a queue. It eats interval stats, handle time, and shrinkage. It is not a feature of a Teams user policy.
  • Shrinkage — The fraction of paid time that is not available for contacts: leave, training, meetings, breaks.
  • Occupancy — Handle time divided by handle time plus available time. High occupancy feels efficient and burns people out.
  • Service level — The fraction of contacts answered inside a target, for example 80 percent in 20 seconds. Erlang C is one teaching model of it.
  • Power BI and CQD — Power BI can sit on top of Call Quality Dashboard data. It does not replace Call Analytics for one call, and it inherits a bad subnet map.
  • Screen pop — The CRM opens the contact that matches the caller. Teams does not do this by itself for an ordinary PSTN call.
  • E.164 — The international form of a number, starting with + and a country code. Routes and CRM lookups should use it after normalization.
  • Call-ID — The SIP header that identifies one dialog. It is the join key between an SBC capture, a carrier ticket, and a CRM note.
  • Integration seam — A named place where another system is allowed to attach: a policy, a queue overflow, a subnet list, an event, an identifier.
  • Human escape — A digit or intent that always reaches a person. Voice agents need one. Emergency numbers need a real route, not a bot.
  • Certificate expiry seam — The date the SBC certificate stops being valid. It is an integration hook because every carrier and every proxy will notice it on the same morning.
  • Subnet as a shared list — The same subnet definitions should feed emergency location, location-based routing, and the CQD building report.
  • Queue interval — A 15-minute or 30-minute bucket of offered, answered, and abandoned contacts. WFM and Erlang C both assume you know the bucket.
  • Abandon — A caller who hangs up before an agent answers. Whether short abandons count is a business rule.
  • Average handle time — Talk time plus after-call work, unless your definition says otherwise. Erlang traffic is calls times handle time divided by the interval.
  • PIDF-LO — A location object that can be carried in a SIP INVITE for an emergency call so the far end sees where the caller is.
  • On-site notification — An emergency calling policy can notify people on site when an emergency call is placed.
  • GCC High SIP — Office 365 GCC High uses sip.pstnhub.gov.teams.microsoft.us. Do not copy the commercial three-proxy list into a government tenant.
  • DoD SIP — Office 365 DoD uses sip.pstnhub.dod.teams.microsoft.us.
  • IPv6 SIP proxies — The plan page also lists sip.ipv6.pstnhub.microsoft.com and the sip2 and sip3 IPv6 names, resolving into 2603:1063:400::/40.
  • Early offer — Teams Direct Routing presents media in the INVITE. An SBC or CUBE that waits for a delayed offer will negotiate badly.
  • AudioCodes — A certified SBC family. Mediant devices and their management tools are common in Direct Routing designs. The Microsoft certified list is the support boundary, the vendor guide is the config.
  • Ribbon SBC — Ribbon’s certified session border controllers are a Direct Routing option. Core and software editions differ.
  • Oracle SBC — Oracle Communications Session Border Controller can be certified for Direct Routing on specific releases. Check the Microsoft list for the release, not the logo.
  • Cisco CUBE — Cisco Unified Border Element on IOS-XE, when the release is certified, is a normal Direct Routing SBC and the usual bridge to CUCM.
  • Vendor-first support — Microsoft’s Direct Routing support boundary: you call the SBC vendor, they escalate to Microsoft if they must. Non-certified SBCs can be refused.
  • Analog via Direct Routing — Fax, door phones, and elevators usually stay on an SBC or SIP Gateway, not on a Teams client.
  • Presence-based routing — A queue can skip agents whose Teams presence says they are busy or in a meeting.
  • Opt out of a queue — Agents can be allowed to leave a queue. If you forbid it, staffing math must include breaks or they will dodge in other ways.
  • Callback — A queue feature that calls the customer back. The prerequisite article treats callback as a case that needs funding on the resource account.
  • Graph communications — Microsoft Graph APIs used by Extend and by recording and presence integrations. They are not a SIP trunk.
  • Policy recording bot join — The recorder joins as an application because the user’s policy names it. If the bot fails to join, you have a policy, an ID, or a media problem, in that order.
  • Retention of recordings — Compliance recordings are retained by the partner’s system, not by Teams meeting storage. Convenience recordings follow meeting storage.
  • Evaluation form — The QM scorecard. It should cite behaviors you can hear, not vibes.
  • Screen recording — Often a QM vendor feature on the agent desktop. Teams compliance recording’s documented media list is voice, video, and screen share inside the Teams session, which is not the same as a silent desktop recorder.
  • Building map — The table that says which subnet is which building in CQD. It is only as good as the subnets you typed.
  • Admin roles for CQD — CQD is an admin report, not a personal call history. The people who can open it are the roles you grant, not every user.
  • After-call work — Time the agent spends in the CRM after the caller hangs up. Include it in handle time if your WFM definition says so.
  • Skill-based routing — Assignment that prefers a representative who has the skill the classification step wrote on the work item.
  • Intent-based routing — Classification that uses an intent, often from a bot or a model, to pick a queue.
  • Proactive outbound — Contact-center campaigns that place calls outward. They need a number, a consent story, and a capacity plan.
  • Real-time translation — A channel feature that can translate a conversation. It is not a substitute for a language queue when the policy says a human must speak the language.
  • Knowledge source — The documents a Copilot or a generative agent is allowed to quote. If the source is wrong, the answer is wrong with confidence.
  • Generative orchestration — The Copilot Studio mode that chooses topics and tools from the conversation, as opposed to a rigid topic tree.
  • Classic topics — The menu-like Copilot Studio design. Predictable and easier to test. Weaker on paraphrase.
  • Event Grid — One way ACS delivers call events to your app. If the subscription is wrong, your IVR never sees RecognizeCompleted.
  • DTMF relay — Digits in the audio path or as telephone-events. IVRs fail when the SBC strips them.
  • Comfort noise and SID — Silence suppression changes the average bitrate. Do not size a link only from a G.711 average that assumed comfort noise you did not enable.
  • Packetization 20 ms — The usual voice packet interval in these bandwidth figures. A 10 ms interval doubles the header tax.
  • Jitter buffer — The client waits a little so late packets can still be played. Too small and you hear gaps. Too large and you talk over each other.
  • Round-trip time — How long a packet takes to go and come back. Long RTT with low loss means distance or hairpin, not a dirty cable.
  • DSCP rewrite — A router or VPN that sets the mark back to 0. The client did its job and the path undid it.
  • Browser join ports — People who join meetings in a browser do not use the fixed 50000-range client ports. An ACL that only matches those ports misses them.
  • Mac and mobile DSCP — Mac, iOS, and Android use hard-coded audio EF and a hard-coded video and sharing mark. They are not the Windows policy object.
  • ms-teams.exe — The current Teams client process name used when you bind a Windows QoS policy. An old policy aimed at the classic client marks nothing.
  • Busy hour — The hour you actually size. Average day divided by eight is not a busy hour.
  • Dual running — Both PBX and Teams carry live numbers during a move. Rollback needs the old route left intact.
  • Number porting — Moving a public number from one carrier to another. It has a date and a letter of authority. It is not a PowerShell metaphor.
  • Caller ID policy — What outbound calls present. Resource-account outbound and user outbound are different policies.
  • Music on hold — The audio a queue plays while a caller waits. Licensing and the file both matter, and silence is a choice you should make on purpose.
  • Voicemail overflow — A respectable overflow target when nobody is left to answer. It depends on Cloud Voicemail and a mailbox for the account that owns it.
  • Pilot number — The public number on the first voice app. Everything else can be an internal target.
  • Security group policy assign — Compliance recording and many Teams policies can be assigned to a group. A user in the wrong group is recorded, or not, as a consequence.
  • Effective policy — The policy the user actually has after direct assignment and group assignment are resolved. The one you edited may not be it.
  • Lab tenant — A tenant that is not production. Pair a lab SBC there. Do not discover certificate behavior on the company trunk.
  • Get-Help before Set — Cmdlet parameters move between MicrosoftTeams module versions. Read help in the version you are about to run.
  • Least privilege — Use the telephony or communications role that can do the task. Global Administrator is not a voice habit.
  • Service number — A number assigned to a resource account or conferencing, as opposed to a user’s personal number. The inventory and the license follow the connectivity option.
  • Unassigned number treatment — What Teams does with a number you own that is not assigned. Routing it to an attendant is a product choice you should make.
  • SIP options timer teaching model — This campus treats repeated missed OPTIONS as a reason to pull a trunk. It does not publish Microsoft’s internal counter as if it were a documented integer.
  • Failover rehearsal — Pull the primary proxy or the primary SBC in a window and prove the next one takes calls.
  • Codec intersection empty — If the SBC and Teams offer no codec in common, the call fails, often with 488.
  • G.711 law — A-law and μ-law are both G.711. A trunk that offers the wrong one still often interoperates, and sometimes does not. Offer both if the carrier is vague.
  • Comfort message — The words the queue plays about position or wait. A lie (‘your call is important’) is still a design choice you can decline.
  • Recording disclosure — Both convenience and compliance recording require participant notification. Compliance does not ask the participant for consent in the comparison Microsoft publishes.
  • Do not record emergency — Know whether your recorder and your policy include emergency calls, and follow the rule you were given. Do not route emergency calls into a bot so they can be ‘handled.’
  • CRM timeline — The case note should carry the Call-ID, the number in E.164, the queue, and the agent. Then a later complaint can be found.
  • Future hook — A seam left on purpose for a system you might add later, using a product that already exists. It is not a promise that Microsoft will ship a feature.
  • Power model wording — Older conversation says Power for the third contact-center model. The Learn article maintained on 14 October 2026 says Unify, Extend, and Connect.
  • Certified list changes — The list of SBCs and contact-center solutions changes. A campus page is not the list.
  • User transfer license — In the Connect model, Microsoft’s documentation says the contact-center side does not need a phone-system license, and the Teams user who receives a transferred call does need Teams Phone and a number.
  • Trial is not a design — A Dynamics or Teams trial proves a click path. It does not prove emergency calling, recording retention, or a busy hour.
  • Rollback — The numbers go back to the old route if the new path fails. If you deleted the old route, you do not have a rollback.
  • Evidence pack — Call-ID, the policy you read, the ladder, and the CQD or Call Analytics row. One pack per hypothesis.
  • Contoso sample — A fictional tenant name used in Microsoft samples and in this campus. It is not a customer.
  • Exam sandbox — Microsoft’s practice UI for what an exam looks like. It is not the product lab.
  • Renewal assessment — Role-based certifications expire unless you pass the free renewal on Learn. Retirement is a different event from expiry.
  • Study guide wins — When this campus and a study guide disagree, the study guide accessed the week you sit the exam wins.
  • Session border controller — The device that speaks SIP to Teams and SIP or another protocol to the carrier or PBX, and enforces the edge.
  • SIP ALG — A firewall feature that rewrites SIP. It breaks Direct Routing more often than it helps.
  • Public IP on the SBC — Teams must be able to reach the SBC. A purely private address with no public path will not pair.
  • DNS TTL and proxies — Proxy addresses change inside the published ranges. A firewall object built from one lookup goes stale.
  • Mutual TLS option — The plan page says that if you enable mutual TLS, install the Microsoft trusted roots on the SBC.
  • Media bypass flag — The gateway setting that allows media to skip Microsoft’s processors. It is not implied by a short ping.
  • Processor path — The default media path through Microsoft media processors in the region closest to the call.
  • Region of media — Media processors exist in multiple regions. Calls prefer a close one. You do not pick a street address for the processor.
  • Voice user disable — A user can be licensed and still not be enabled for Enterprise Voice in the way the tenant expects. Read the user before you test.
  • Calling policy — A Teams calling policy controls client calling features. It is not the voice routing policy that selects an SBC.
  • Caller ID substitution — Presenting a service number instead of a personal mobile. It is a policy, not a hope.
  • Normalization translation tokens — Teams translations use tokens such as $1 for a capture group. The first matching rule applies the translation and stops.
  • Extension dialing — A short rule can turn a 4-digit extension into E.164. It must not run before emergency codes.
  • Holiday call flow — The attendant’s holiday set replaces business hours for those dates.
  • Operator zero — The attendant’s operator is where zero, and many failures, should land.
  • Dial by name scope — Directory search should list only the people you intend callers to reach.
  • Agent list order — Serial routing uses the order of the agent list. Round robin does not.
  • Queue timeout seconds — How long the caller waits before overflow. Too short and overflow is the product. Too long and they abandon.
  • Greeting rights — The audio file must be one you have the right to play. A popular song is not a free hold track.
  • After hours target — Where calls go when the attendant is closed. Voicemail, another number, or a message.
  • Network site object — A named place in the Teams network topology. Subnets attach to it. Emergency addresses and LBR attach to that story.
  • Emergency address validation — Civic addresses are validated. An unvalidated string is not a location you can trust.
  • Wireless location caution — Wi-Fi clients can roam across subnets that do not match the building you think. The subnet you mapped is the location you will send.
  • SBA registration — Clients must be able to use the appliance when the cloud path is gone. An appliance with no client configuration is a spare server.
  • Local SBC during survival — The SBA still needs a PSTN path. That is the local SBC and carrier, not a cloud media processor you cannot reach.
  • Teams Rooms QoS — Rooms need the same DSCP plan as PCs, using the rooms guidance for Windows and Android.
  • Common area identity — The phone signs in as its own account. Voicemail and caller ID follow that account.
  • Device conditional access — A policy that requires a compliant interactive PC will block a phone or a room.
  • Firmware on the list — Certification is specific about software. ‘We own AudioCodes’ is not a version.
  • SRTP profile mismatch — Both sides must agree the media is SRTP. A gateway forced to RTP/AVP will not complete a normal Direct Routing call.
  • Telephone-event — The SDP way digits are carried so an IVR can see them even when the audio codec would hide tones.
  • Call-ID header — The header that stays with a SIP dialog so two logs can be proved to be one call.
  • From and To headers — Who the request claims to be, and who it is for. A normalization bug shows up here as a duplicated country code.
  • SDP offer — The media description inside the INVITE when early offer is used. Codecs, ports, and SRTP live here.
  • Jitter over 30 ms teaching clue — A commonly taught CQD clue is to treat sustained jitter around 30 ms as painful. Confirm the current data dictionary before you quote a threshold as policy.
  • Loss around 10 percent — Packet loss near 10 percent is a widely used teaching line for a poor stream. Verify the current CQD definition before you automate an alert on it.
  • Subnet dimension — CQD’s subnet field is only useful if you built the building map from real subnets.
  • Work item — The unit unified routing assigns: a call, a chat, a case. It is not a Teams message.
  • Queue membership — Representatives receive work only from queues they belong to.
  • Security role in Dynamics — The Dataverse role that lets a representative open the app and touch the rows they should.
  • Embedded versus standalone — Contact Center can sit inside Customer Service or stand as its own app. Agents must be told which one they open.
  • Intent unknown bucket — The classification result you get when the model or the rules do not know. It needs a queue.
  • Copilot summary — A draft of the conversation for the representative. It can be wrong. The representative still owns what is sent.
  • Knowledge article freshness — The date on the source the bot is allowed to quote. Retired prices stay retired only if you remove them.
  • Fallback topic — The classic-topic path when nothing else matches. Empty fallback is silence.
  • DTMF zero — The usual human escape. It has to be implemented, not merely printed on a slide.
  • Transfer target license — The Teams user who receives a PSTN transfer needs Teams Phone and a number in the Connect model’s description.
  • Multitenant SBC caution — Some Connect providers share SBC infrastructure across customers. Your routing must still isolate your numbers.
  • Letter of authority — The carrier document that allows a number to move. Porting without it does not happen.
  • Rollback route — The old path left in place so numbers can return. Deleting it is not a cleanup. It is the removal of the safety.
  • Group policy assignment — Many Teams policies, including compliance recording, can target a group. Membership changes the effective policy without a click on the user.
  • Zero-cost allotment — Teams Phone subscriptions include a number of free resource-account licenses you still have to acquire in the admin center.
  • At least one Teams Phone — The prerequisite article says the tenant needs Teams Phone licensing for the voice-app services to exist.
  • Do not share global admin — A shared Global Administrator account erases the audit of who changed a route.
  • Sample 555 numbers — Use 555 numbers and example domains in labs. They are not a customer directory.
  • Renewal versus retirement — A certification you forget to renew expires for you. A retirement means Microsoft withdrew the exam. They are different calendars.
  • Official practice assessment — Microsoft’s practice questions, when published, match the exam more honestly than a random PDF.
  • Busy-hour interval — The half hour you staff, not the daily average divided into imaginary hours.
  • Adherence — Whether the agent was in the state the schedule promised. It needs a real state feed from the contact center.
  • Service level target — A pair: a percent and a number of seconds, plus the abandon rule. ‘Eighty twenty’ means nothing until both are defined.
  • Hold music licensing — The hold file is a copyrighted work unless you made it or licensed it.
  • Notification group — The people the emergency policy emails or messages when an emergency call starts.
  • Dispatchable location — A location specific enough for a responder to find the caller. A city name is not enough for a campus.
  • Carrier test number — The number or process the carrier gave you for emergency tests. It is not the public emergency number.
  • Split tunnel — VPN configuration that sends only corporate apps through the tunnel and lets Teams media go direct.
  • Port mirroring — A switch copy of packets so you can see DSCP at the egress without standing on the user’s keyboard.
  • ACL mark — A router rule that sets DSCP based on the port range, useful for devices that will not mark for themselves.
  • Two directions — A call has media each way. Sizing one direction and forgetting the other undersizes a symmetric policer.
  • G.722 header tax — G.722 at 160 bytes every 20 ms pays the same header tax as G.711. Wideband does not remove IP headers.
  • Comfort noise sizing — Silence suppression lowers the average and not the peak. Size the peak.
  • Pilot rollback week — A quiet week on the new path before you delete the old trunk.
  • Expert wave — Move people who call each other in the same migration wave so they do not trombone for a year.
  • IVR range split — Contact-center numbers stay on UCCE. User numbers go to Teams. The SBC enforces the split.
  • Whisper and barge caution — Supervisor features such as whisper and barge belong to the contact-center product, not to a Teams peer call.
  • Recording both sides — If the contact center and Teams both record, you still need one Call-ID to know it is the same conversation.
  • Privacy review — Recording, desktop capture, and directory search all need a privacy decision before go-live.
  • Restore test — A retention setting you have never restored is a hope. Ask the recording partner for one file back.
  • Form the agent saw — Quality scores should use a form published to the people being scored.
  • Export for WFM — Queue totals have to be able to leave the contact center on a schedule the WFM tool can read.
  • Shrinkage labeled — If shrinkage is a guess, say it is a guess. Thirty percent is not a law of nature.
  • Occupancy watch — High occupancy next to rising abandons means the service level is being spent to look efficient.
  • Case timeline fields — E.164, Call-ID, queue, agent. Four fields make a case auditable.
  • Bot eval set — A list of utterances whose correct intent you already know. Run it before you publish a prompt change.
  • Unannounced feature risk — A design that needs a product Microsoft has not shipped is not a hook. It is a bet.
  • Admin center SBC status — The Direct Routing page that shows whether Teams considers the SBC healthy.
  • Test call both ways — Inbound and outbound are different routes. A successful outbound call does not prove the carrier can deliver inbound.
  • Emergency both ways — You care that the emergency call leaves, and that the location and notification are correct. Inbound to the queue is a different test.
  • Holiday and certificate calendar — Two dates that take companies down: the holiday the attendant does not know, and the certificate that expires.
  • Contoso is fictional — Contoso, Fabrikam, and 555 numbers in this campus are samples. They are not your tenant.
  • Checked date — 14 October 2026 is when this campus was compared with Learn. It is not a promise the page stays still.
